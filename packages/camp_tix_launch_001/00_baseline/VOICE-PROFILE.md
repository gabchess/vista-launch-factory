## Apply to writing

Start with a familiar buying situation. Prefer everyday verbs such as find, check, set and ask. Keep the item and the reader's decision concrete. Explain one action at a time, with enough connected prose that the article reads naturally aloud.

Tix can sound playful in a short attributed line such as “Sup, I’m Tix.” Use that personality sparingly in the article. The surrounding product-team prose should be calm and useful. First-person Tix lines must remain clearly attributed; the writer does not become an actual customer or Gabe.

Use “your limit” when discussing the buyer's chosen amount. Reserve market-value language for a claim supported by the selected evidence. Put engineering terms such as x402 in context only when they help the reader understand the product direction. Avoid unsupported superlatives, invented success stories, urgency, personal earnings and arbitrary performance statistics.

## Editorial checks for the first article

- Every factual statement maps to the selected ledger, including any qualifier.
- The opening gives the reader a concrete situation and a reason to continue.
- The copy keeps Tix's approachable character without turning every paragraph into a mascot monologue.
- Planned behavior remains visibly planned in the prose.
- The ending uses the approved waitlist CTA without inventing availability or a deadline.

This profile does not assert that Reggie's LinkedIn archive has been retrieved or that Vista house style is calibrated. Those expression sources belong to the later client campaign.
