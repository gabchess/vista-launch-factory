# Tix launch: source and writing brief

Version 1. Product: Tixmancer. Campaign: `camp_tix_launch_001`. Actual reviewer: Gabe.

## Reader and purpose

Help a person shopping for secondhand gear understand what Tix is being built to do and decide whether to follow its development. The concrete example is a buyer looking for a used Sony camera in Brooklyn for less than $950. That amount belongs to the example; it is not a product limit or price.

The first article should explain the buyer's decision: describe the item, set a personal limit, inspect a candidate and decide what to pursue. It can introduce the direction shown in the accepted film, with clear language about work in progress. The article must preserve the distinction between an asking price below a personal limit and evidence that an item is below market value.

Publisher: Tixmancer. Use the product team's voice for an article. Attribute first-person character lines to Tix. Do not invent a founder experience, a customer testimonial or a purchase outcome.

## Shared creative decisions

- Retain the Tix character, the garage-sale-flipper positioning and a direct, lightly playful tone.
- Preserve the accepted 10-second animation and 55-second product film. They are creative references; neither establishes a new runtime capability.
- Keep the exact CTA: **Join the waitlist** followed by **tixmancer.xyz**. Its intended destination is `https://tixmancer.xyz/`. The current website's collection flow needs verification before publication; an unverified `/waitlist` URL must not be substituted.
- Brand treatment follows the accepted creative: Tix blue `#0052FF`, white `#FFFFFF`, paper `#F7F5F2`, Inter and JetBrains Mono. A new visual rendition receives its own review.
- Sources establish claims. Voice examples guide expression. The client demonstration later uses Prospector source material and its selected Vista/Reggie expression references in a separate campaign.

## First writer's assignment

After Gabe reviews this exact baseline, produce a complete blog article with title, summary, body, metadata and CTA. Write a coherent reader-facing piece; keep claim IDs and technical evidence in its companion review file. Use only the permitted formulations in `claim-ledger.json`. Qualify product direction as planned work, and omit held claims.

The article introduces Tix and invites interest. A shipped release date, production availability, current marketplace coverage, pricing, customer count, funding outcome and purchase success have not been established for this campaign. Do not fill those gaps with assumptions.
