The Work announcement starts with availability and access, then explains a recognizable work problem. It moves through changes, component walkthroughs, a compact scenario and audience-specific guidance. Screenshots appear beside the relevant explanation. Its closing gives existing customers a concrete in-product action and new readers a separate entry point. [Work launch](https://vistasocial.com/insights/vista-work-social-media-project-management/).

Listening addresses the reader and their team directly. Headings describe actions or decisions. Short paragraphs explain what the user sees, followed by lists of useful details. The closing names the navigation path and a small first setup task. Apply that operational specificity to the Tix source. [Listening launch](https://vistasocial.com/insights/introducing-advanced-social-listening/).

The AI-media launch uses a named founder's perspective, then demonstrates capabilities through prompts, images and video. It discusses workflow consequences and rollout details. Its reported timings, plan availability, testimonials and first-person experiences belong to that source; they supply no Tix evidence. [AI-media launch](https://vistasocial.com/insights/ai-image-and-video-generation/).

The process article opens on a familiar task, answers reader questions and works through a specific handoff. It qualifies research evidence and explains where a broader tool still fits. Use that explanatory depth when it helps the reader understand Tix. Its FAQ is a format option; a launch article need not inherit the whole section. [Process article](https://vistasocial.com/insights/social-media-project-management/).

## Apply the voice

Write to a person doing the work. Prefer a concrete action, screen, question or consequence over an abstract benefit. Use ordinary verbs and contractions. Keep paragraphs easy to scan, then vary sentence length so the article reads aloud naturally.

Use the product-editor register for this draft. The first person requires evidence about the actual publisher. Gabe's prose constraints remain in force: avoid em dashes, repeated rhetorical reversals and stacked punch lines even where an example uses them. This is an editorial adaptation of the observed sample.

## Recommended article shape

1. Name the product and the supported change or current availability.
2. Establish the reader's task and the friction that matters.
3. Explain the supported workflow through a few useful sections.
4. Place the relevant product view beside its explanation.
5. Add one grounded example and necessary capability boundaries.
6. Close with the actual next action from the Tix brief.

This compact structure combines the Work and Listening patterns. Keep the body as long as the evidence needs; the site's displayed reading labels do not prescribe a word count. A waitlist CTA must not inherit Vista's free-trial terms.
