## Verbatim excerpt

Social media moves fast. Your tools should move with it.

But a lot of teams I've talked to lately are realizing their social media management platform has quietly stopped keeping pace. The complaints are remarkably consistent, and they keep pointing at one specific name: Hootsuite.

This isn't about one frustrated customer. It's a pattern. Here are the five reasons users keep citing when they decide to leave.

1. Price hikes that show up on the invoice, not in your inbox

The frustration isn't that Hootsuite raises prices—most SaaS companies do. It's that the increases tend to land mid-contract, without notice, and at amounts that are hard to explain. Plenty of users describe opening an invoice to find their rate has doubled or tripled overnight.

[…]

2. Annual contracts that won't let you go

Hootsuite's contracts auto-renew for a full year unless you send written cancellation notice at least 60 days out. Miss that window—by even a day—and you're committed for another twelve months.

[…]

The trouble is that the moment most teams actually reassess their tools (when something breaks, a better option appears, or budget season hits) almost always lands inside that closed window. You realize you want to leave, and then you realize you can't. Not without paying for a year you don't plan to use.

[…]

For an agency mid-campaign, that isn't a minor inconvenience. The client doesn't know your tool is down and doesn't care. Support that's hard to reach in those moments isn't a frustration—it's a liability.
