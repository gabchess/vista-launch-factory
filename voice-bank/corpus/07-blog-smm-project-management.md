## Verbatim excerpt

> Count the tabs open on your second monitor. Posts live in Vista Social, while the work about them lives in Asana. You sit in the middle, copying a comment into a card and explaining which client it belongs to.
>
> Both tools may be doing their jobs. The friction sits in the handoff between them. Each time a post, comment, review, or report becomes a task elsewhere, somebody has to describe the work again.
>
> Then come the questions neither tab can answer alone: Did this post publish, and did anyone respond to the message? The social media project management setup looks tidy, but the SMM still has to piece together the full story.
>
> That repeated explanation is the translation tax. It takes time from content work and grows with every client, profile, and teammate.
>
> **A task works differently when it carries the social item that created it.**

> ### The short version
> - **Switching adds up:** Harvard’s 2026 study counted 814 daily app changes for the median employee.
> - **What changes:** A social-native task keeps a live route back to the post, conversation, review, or report behind it.
> - **Wider scope:** General project tools still suit cross-department planning, staffing, and Gantt timelines.
> - **Start small:** Move one recurring handoff and count how many explanations disappear.
