# Tone & Style Brief: Vista Social Voice Bank (INTERIM per ADR 0015)

**Status: INTERIM.** Derived only from the public corpus in `corpus/` plus the Barry email seed. Replace or revise when Vista's real brand guide arrives. Every observation cites corpus filenames.

---

## 1. Sentence length & rhythm

- **Short declaratives stacked for punch.** "One workspace. One login. One source of truth." (`08-blog-introducing-vista-work.md`). "Not anymore." as a standalone paragraph (`15-social-x-ai-image-video.md`). "No hunting." (`08`).
- **The pain-scene → pivot → product three-beat.** Barry email seed: three stacked rhetorical questions, then a one-line pivot naming what the task lacks, then the Vista Work fix (`01-email-barry-approval-queue.md`, paraphrased). The SMM-project-management blog opens identically: "Count the tabs open on your second monitor." → "That repeated explanation is the translation tax." → bolded pivot sentence (`07-blog-smm-project-management.md`).
- **Longer, multi-clause sentences appear in body/blog prose but stay plain**, subordination is used for cause-and-effect, never for flourish (`06-blog-vista-social-vs-later.md`, `16-social-x-hootsuite-five-reasons.md`).
- **Rhetorical questions are a signature device**, in the seed email (short stacked which-version questions; see `01`, paraphrased) and in social ("Does it get any better? 😌", `14-social-x-3-features.md`; "Using AI just for captions? Boring.", `13`).

## 2. Vocabulary register

- **Plain operator English, not marketingese.** "hunting for it", "copying context between them", "the second tool is gone", "work finally lives where it happens" (`01`, `08`, `03-website-vista-work-page.md`).
- **A few coined, memorable labels**: "translation tax" (`07`), "Cut the Suite" (`18-linkedin-company-cut-the-suite.md`), "AI Social Stack" (`13`).
- **Product nouns stay exact**: Vista Work, Ask Vista, Composer, Inbox, Profile Groups, My Work (`06`, `08`, `10-blog-ai-image-video-generation.md`).
- **Competitors are named directly** (Hootsuite, Later) with a calm, evidence-led takedown tone, "This isn't about one frustrated customer. It's a pattern." (`16`, `06`).
- **"legacy platforms" is a recurring us-vs-them frame** (`02-website-homepage.md`, `04-website-publishing-page.md`).

## 3. Opener patterns

- **Homepage/product pages: benefit-as-headline, often with attitude.** "That other tab where you manage your work? Closed." (`03`). "Your brand, impossible to ignore on social media" (`02`).
- **Blog posts: open inside the reader's pain scene, second person, present tense.** "Count the tabs open on your second monitor." (`07`). "Every social team runs the same split: content lives in Vista Social, but the work about that content lives somewhere else." (`08`).
- **Launch posts: straight announcement, first-person proud.** "Today, I'm proud to announce…" (`10`); "Today we're launching…" (`09-blog-advanced-social-listening.md`).
- **The Barry email seed opens with the reader's exact moment of confusion**: an approval task that names a post without showing it, followed immediately by which-version questions (`01`, paraphrased), no greeting, no product name until the fix.

## 4. Emoji & punctuation habits

- **Website/email: near-zero emoji**; the arrow "→" is the CTA punctuation of record ("Start your free trial →", "Open Vista Work →", "Playbook →") (`01`, `02`, `04`, `13`).
- **Social: light, functional emoji**, ✅ 👇 😌 🖼️ 🎬 📅 ✂️ 👀 ➡️, one per beat, never clusters (`12` to `15`, `18`).
- **Bold used for the one-sentence thesis** in blog posts: "**A task works differently when it carries the social item that created it.**" (`07`); "**The bottleneck moved from making content to catching the moment.**" (`09`).
- **Bullet glyphs: "•" in X posts, "→" in LinkedIn lists, "- **Label:**" in blog "short version" blocks** (`13`, `18`, `06`, `07`).
- Casual lowercase sentence-starts appear on LinkedIn ("too many teams are stuck…", "because modern social media management should actually feel modern.") (`18`), a deliberate conversational break, not present on the website.

## 5. CTA style

- **One imperative link line.** "Open Vista Work →" (`01`). "Start your free trial →" / "Request a demo" (`02`, `04`, `05-website-analytics-page.md`). "Cut the suite here ➡️" (`18`).
- **Friction-removal reassurance travels with website CTAs**: "No credit card required / Free trial available / Cancel anytime" (`04`, `05`).
- **Email seed CTA is exactly two words plus an arrow**, no "click here", no exclamation (`01`).
- Blog CTAs are softer: "Read more on our blog." (`16`); "Start small: Move one recurring handoff…" (`07`).

## 6. Humor level

- **Dry, deadpan, understated, never jokey.** "Using AI just for captions? Boring." (`13`). "That other tab where you manage your work? Closed." (`03`). "Does it get any better? 😌" (`14`).
- The blog seeds humor into product guidance: "adding a joke to make your posts more memorable than straightforward marketing messages" (`04`).
- Reggie in person is warmer and self-deprecating, the car-trunk Zoom story, "Maybe they were too scared to" (`22-podcast-reggie-plugged-in-ai-agents.md`), but that register does not appear in company copy.

## 7. How they reference customers & pain points

- **Second person, present tense, concrete artifacts**: tabs, invoices, DMs, approval queues, "a card describing it" (`01`, `07`, `16`). The pain is always a *scene the reader has lived*.
- **Pain is structural, not emotional**: handoffs, translation tax, context switching, mid-contract price hikes, named and mechanized, then dissolved by the product (`07`, `16`, `06`).
- **Social proof is badge + logo ledgers** (G2 #1, "Trusted by teams at" ClickUp/NVIDIA/Oracle) rather than long testimonials (`02`, `04`).
- **Respect for the reader's intelligence**: comparison posts concede the competitor's real strengths ("Later remains a strong choice for creators") (`06`).

## 8. Register differences: blog vs email vs social vs Reggie

- **Email (Barry seed, `01`)**: most restrained. No emoji, no exclamation marks, no greeting/sign-off. Opens mid-confusion, asks short questions, states the cost in one plain sentence (the search outlasting the decision), then the fix in two plain sentences, then a two-word arrow CTA. Closest thing to a person thinking out loud with the reader.
- **Blog (`06` to `10`)**: pain scene → "short version" bullets → sectioned argument → bolded thesis sentences. Authoritative, comparative, still conversational. CEO-bylined posts use first person ("That's the gap I kept coming back to", `10`).
- **Website (`02` to `05`)**: headline attitude + subhead promise + reassurance trio + arrow CTAs. Shortest sentences of any channel.
- **X/LinkedIn (`12` to `19`)**: hooks first ("Everything is a task now. ✅"), emoji as rhythm markers, listicles, hashtags only on X/LinkedIn promo posts, occasional lowercase informality on LinkedIn.
- **Reggie spoken (`21`, `22`)**: warm, story-first, self-deprecating, values-driven ("have them leave… better than I found them"), heavy on practical examples, dismissive of hustle culture ("waking up early has nothing to do with it"). Reggie-written blog (`11`, 2023) is noticeably more formal/corporate than today's company voice, treat as dated.
- **Reggie profile voice (`20`)**: pipe-separated keyword headline, functional not expressive.

## 9. Barry-email-specific traits (the seed, `01`)

1. Opens on the reader's screen, quoting their UI back at them ("a task that says 'Approve the next post.'").
2. Follows with stacked short questions the reader can't answer, building the pain without adjectives.
3. Names the hidden cost in one plain sentence (the search outlasting the decision).
4. Introduces the product late, as relief, not as hero: "In Vista Work, the task and social posts are linked."
5. Concrete contrast pairs: "the actual content, not a card describing it", "see what needs a decision instead of hunting for it first."
6. Zero emoji, zero exclamation points, zero hype words.
7. CTA = product name + arrow, one line, imperative: "Open Vista Work →".
8. "someone, probably you", direct, mildly conspiratorial address of the reader's actual workload.

## Voice rules for adapters

Check any generated copy against these 10 rules (INTERIM per ADR 0015):

1. **DO open with the reader's pain scene, not the product.** The product name should not appear before the problem is felt. (Seed: `01`; blog: `07`, `08`.)
2. **DO quote the reader's own reality back at them**, their UI text, their tabs, their invoice, in concrete nouns. No abstract pain ("struggling with inefficiency" is banned). (`01`, `07`, `16`.)
3. **DON'T use hype adjectives** (amazing, revolutionary, game-changing, seamless* in body copy). Claims are stated flat and proven with specifics. (*"seamlessly" appears once in CEO launch copy, `10`, tolerance: launch announcements only.)
4. **DO write short declarative sentences for pivots** ("Not anymore.", "No hunting.", "Work finally lives where it happens."). Long sentences only carry causal logic. (`08`, `15`, `03`.)
5. **DO use at most one rhetorical-question cluster per piece** to build the pain, then answer it with the product. (`01`, `13`.)
6. **DO make the CTA one imperative line ending in →** (email/website) or one link line (social). Never "Click here!", never multiple competing CTAs in one beat. (`01`, `02`, `18`.)
7. **DON'T use emoji in email or website body copy.** On social, one functional emoji per beat maximum (✅ 👇 📅 style), never decorative clusters. (`01`, `02` vs `13` to `15`.)
8. **DO bold exactly one thesis sentence per long-form piece**, the mechanism claim, e.g. "**A task works differently when it carries the social item that created it.**" (`07`, `09`.)
9. **DO name competitors and their real strengths before their weaknesses**; calm, pattern-based, evidence-led ("It's a pattern", concede-then-contrast). Never sneer. (`06`, `16`.)
10. **DO keep humor dry and deadpan, one beat max**, a flat mic-drop line ("Boring.", "Closed."), not jokes. Reggie's warmer spoken humor (`22`) is for podcast/quotes only, not adapters writing company copy.
11. **DO use "you/your" for the reader and "we" only for Vista as builder** ("Today we're launching…", `09`); avoid "our users" abstractions, say "social teams", "agencies", "your approvers". (`01`, `16`.)
12. **DON'T invent product claims, pricing, or numbers.** Only reuse figures present in the corpus (e.g., "up to 60% lift in reach", `04`; "30,000 brands and agencies", `04`) and never extend them.
